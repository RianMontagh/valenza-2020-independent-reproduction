# Partial independent B12 central-reach observation

The annotation was drawn on this project's own 1995, 2011, 2013 and 2015
annual composites on 12 October 2026. The large-domain images show a southern
course in 1995 and a northern course by 2011. The event polygon surrounds the
observed relocated central reach and adjacent exposed surfaces, from the
known site vicinity toward the northeast. No published figure or author mask
was used to draw these vertices or select the image crops.

This is a deliberately partial central reach. The northeastern end at pixel
row 1300 cuts across the continuing channel. It does not represent event
completion or a natural sedimentary boundary. Small paleochannels may have
already been present in 1995. A broad parent polygon isolates a segment of
the old southern course, also terminating within the image domain.

Parent observation: 1995. Post-relocation observations: 2010--2017. These
windows are unequal and do not establish initiation or completion dates.
No precision in timing between 1995 and 2010 is claimed. All eight post-years
have valid annual composites throughout the large image domain, but annual
medians can retain spectral artifacts. No exclusion polygon was defensible
from the image sequence alone; absence of an exclusion is not evidence that
every detection was caused by avulsion.

The output is a partial spectral disturbance footprint. Its area ratio is an
unequal-window, truncated-reach diagnostic, not the paper's full-event S_R.
It supplies partial Fig. 2 attempts and must not enter Figures 3, 4 or S3.
The detail crop (590:840, 1450:1650) was selected from the independently
observed central meandering and exposed-surface cluster. The annual plot
years are declared as 2011, 2013, 2015, 2017; original high-resolution
counterparts and their exact matching dates are unavailable.
